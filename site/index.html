A better way to learn

Better grades matter. So does learning that lasts.

Students can spend hours studying and still walk into a test unsure of what they know. Often, the issue is not effort or intelligence—it is method.

From Grades to Growth combines practical academic strategies with deeper questions about purpose, motivation, mindset, technology, and how to think clearly in a complicated world.

This is not a lecture about “being more organized.” Students actively practise strategies, ask questions, and leave with approaches they can use right away.

Learn more effectively

Replace ineffective habits with strategies that build durable learning.

Improve academic performance

Identify practical, often overlooked factors that influence grades.

Differentiation skills

Tell the difference between remembering information and understanding concepts.

Develop beyond school

Connect academic success with purpose, responsibility, and personal growth.

Practical clarity

Students will leave able to answer:

1

Which popular study habits waste time, even when they feel productive?

2

How can I tell whether I actually know something?

3

With the same number of study hours, how can I learn twice as much?

4

What factors are easy but often overlooked for achieving good grades?

5

What is the difference between information and concepts—and why does it matter?

Students will not simply receive a list of tips. They will learn why certain strategies work, practise them during the workshop, and create a clearer approach to their own learning.

Meet the facilitator

Martin Braithwaite

Workshop Facilitator

Martin is an experienced educator and mentor dedicated to helping young people thrive both academically and personally. Over the past decade, he has guided students across Canada, Mainland China, and Macau, teaching within both public and private systems across local and international (IB) curricula. He has taught IB Psychology, university-level business and computer science, and moral empowerment and character development programs.

Having benefited from study skills workshops firsthand as a student, Martin is passionate about facilitating this series to help learners connect practical academic strategies with deeper questions of personal growth and purpose.

“When we connect daily study habits with a deeper sense of purpose, students don’t just achieve better results—they gain the agency to shape their own futures.”
